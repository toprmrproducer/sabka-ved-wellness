Peyadi Krama / Samsarjana Krama in Patna

Samsarjana krama, also called peyadi krama, is the step-by-step diet that follows vamana or virechana. It begins with thin rice gruel (peya) and moves through thicker gruel (vilepi) and soups (yusha) to normal meals over a set number of meals, so that digestion, weakened by cleansing, can recover safely. It is as much a part of Panchakarma as the cleansing itself.

Ask anyone who has finished virechana what they wanted most that evening, and the answer is usually food. Something solid, something tasty, something "normal". The classical texts anticipated exactly that craving and wrote firm rules against giving in to it. After cleansing, they say, the digestive fire is like a tiny flame: feed it a heavy log and it goes out; feed it dry grass, then twigs, then sticks, and it grows back strong. Samsarjana krama is that sequence of grass, twigs and sticks, and skipping it is one of the commonest reasons Panchakarma leaves people weaker than it should.

What Samsarjana Krama means

Samsarjana means bringing together or gradually restoring, and krama means sequence. Peyadi krama means "the sequence beginning with peya", the first food in the series. Together they name the graded diet after shodhana, especially after vamana (therapeutic emesis) and virechana (therapeutic purgation), and in some cases after strong niruha basti or raktamokshana.

Classical description

Charaka Samhita, in the Siddhisthana, describes the sequence in detail, and Ashtanga Hridaya (around 7th century CE) and Sushruta Samhita describe the same principle. The classical sequence is:

  1. Peya: very thin rice gruel, mostly liquid with a few soft grains
  2. Vilepi: thicker gruel, with more grain
  3. Akrita yusha: thin soup of pulses such as moong, without salt, fat or spices
  4. Krita yusha: the same soup, now lightly seasoned with salt, ghee and mild spices
  5. Akrita and krita mamsa rasa: unseasoned, then seasoned meat soup, in the classical texts; for vegetarians, Ayurvedic practice substitutes further stages of seasoned moong soup and soft rice

The number of meals (annakala) at each stage depends on how strong the cleansing was. Classical teaching describes three grades. After pravara (strong) shuddhi, the sequence is spread over about twelve meals, roughly seven days. After madhyama (moderate) shuddhi, about eight meals, roughly five days. After avara (mild) shuddhi, about four meals, roughly three days. Each stage is usually given for one to three meals before moving on, depending on the grade.

Why it is needed: classical logic and modern sense

The Ayurvedic view

Cleansing removes aggravated doshas, but it also leaves agni, the digestive fire, temporarily weak. The texts warn that heavy food on a weak agni produces ama, undigested residue, and can cause indigestion, bloating, weakness and even a return of the original problem. The graded diet rekindles agni step by step, from the lightest food to normal meals, so that strength returns without overload.

What modern research shows

Formal research on samsarjana krama is scarce. A 2021 case study from the All India Institute of Ayurveda, New Delhi, published in the Journal of Ayurveda and Integrative Medicine, described the use of samsarjana krama in a patient with agnimandya (weak digestion) linked to chronic metabolic conditions. Most clinical studies of vamana and virechana, such as a 2011 study on seasonal vamana in spring published in Ayu, and a 2026 protocol studying vamana and virechana in healthy adults, include samsarjana krama as a standard part of the protocol rather than testing it separately. Modern clinical nutrition follows a similar principle in refeeding after fasting, bowel preparation or gastrointestinal illness: start light and liquid, increase gradually. The logic is sound, even if the classical sequence itself has not been formally tested.

Who needs it

  • Everyone who has had vamana or virechana, according to the grade of cleansing
  • Some patients after a strong course of niruha basti or other shodhana, as the doctor advises
  • In a milder form, people recovering from acute indigestion or diarrhoea, under guidance

Cautions

  • Diabetes: blood sugar may fall or fluctuate on a light diet, especially on medicines or insulin. Monitoring and medicine adjustment with your physician are essential. Never change insulin or tablets on your own.
  • Elderly and weak patients: the sequence may be adapted to avoid excessive weakness.
  • Pregnancy and breastfeeding: cleansing is generally not done in pregnancy; consult the doctor about any diet changes.
  • Warning signs: persistent vomiting, severe weakness, dizziness, fainting, very little urine or blood in stools need immediate medical attention.

The exact foods, quantities and timing are prescribed for you. Do not copy a sequence from someone else.

Practical tips for Bihari kitchens

  • Use old (aged) rice if available; it is lighter to digest.
  • Split, husked moong dal is ideal for yusha.
  • A pinch of saunth (dry ginger), jeera and hing can be added only at the stage the doctor allows.
  • Drink warm water in small sips through the day.
  • Keep curd, raw salads, fried food, sweets, chana, rajma, litti, non-vegetarian food and tea or coffee away until advised.
  • Eat only when genuinely hungry, and stop before feeling full.

Rest and routine

Alongside the diet, the texts advise rest: no long travel, no heavy exercise, no daytime sleep, no late nights, no exposure to cold wind, no loud speaking and no sexual activity until the sequence is complete. We help patients from outside Patna plan their stay so that this phase is not spent on a train.

Signs that digestion is recovering

The texts teach the doctor to move forward through the sequence by watching the patient rather than the calendar. Useful signs include real hunger arriving at meal times, a light body, a clean taste in the mouth, comfortable passing of wind and stool, clear urine and a return of energy. Signs that the pace is too fast include heaviness after meals, burping with the taste of food, bloating, a coated tongue, loss of appetite and sluggishness. When those appear, the doctor may hold you at the same stage for another meal or two, or step back. This is why we stay in touch during the sequence, by visit or by phone, instead of handing over a sheet and saying goodbye. Small adjustments at this stage make a large difference to how you feel in the weeks that follow.

Why it matters for value

Samsarjana krama costs very little in money: rice, moong, ghee and patience. But skipping it can waste the time, medicines and effort spent on cleansing. When we explain what a Panchakarma programme includes, the diet plan and follow-up during this phase are part of it, because they are part of the treatment.

After the sequence

Once you return to normal meals, the doctor may advise a period of rasayana, rejuvenating medicines and foods, and a sustainable diet and routine. The aim is not to go back to the habits that created the problem, but to build new ones on a digestion that has had a chance to reset.

FAQ

Questions about Peyadi Krama / Samsarjana Krama

What is Samsarjana Krama?

Samsarjana krama is the graded diet after vamana or virechana. It starts with thin rice gruel and moves step by step through thicker gruel and soups to normal meals.

How long does Samsarjana Krama last?

Classically about 3, 5 or 7 days, depending on whether the cleansing was mild, moderate or strong. The doctor decides after the procedure.

Can I eat normally right after virechana?

No. Heavy food on the day of cleansing can cause indigestion and weakness and undo the benefits. Follow the graded diet.

What if I am vegetarian?

Classical texts include meat soup in the later stages, but vegetarian patients are given extra stages of moong soup and soft rice instead.

I have diabetes. Is the graded diet safe?

It can be done with care, but blood sugar must be monitored and medicines adjusted by your physician. Never change insulin or tablets on your own.

Why is rest important during this diet?

The texts say the body is delicate after cleansing. Travel, exertion, cold and late nights can disturb recovery.
