Netra Tarpana in Patna

Netra tarpana is a classical Ayurvedic eye procedure in which a ring of dough is built around the eye and filled with lukewarm medicated ghee, so that the open eye rests in it for a set time. It is used for dry, tired and strained eyes and as supportive care in some eye conditions, only after an eye examination and alongside, never instead of, an eye specialist's care.

Picture a working day in Patna today: a phone checked before getting out of bed, hours on a laptop or at a coaching-centre whiteboard, the glare of traffic on the way home, and more screen time at night. By evening many people's eyes feel gritty, hot and tired. Ayurveda devoted a whole branch, Shalakya Tantra, to the eyes, ears, nose and throat, and among its eye procedures Netra tarpana is the best known. Its name means "satisfying" or "nourishing" the eye, and that is the feeling it aims for.

What Netra Tarpana is

Netra means eye, and tarpana means nourishing or satiating. In the procedure, a firm dough made of black gram (urad) flour is shaped into a ring and sealed around the eye socket. Lukewarm, liquefied medicated ghee, most often triphala ghrita or a plain ghee preparation the doctor chooses, is poured slowly into the ring until the eyelashes are covered. The patient opens and closes the eyes gently while the ghee rests on them for a set time. The ghee is then drained, and the eyes are cleaned and rested.

On this site, netra basti and akshi tarpana appear on a separate page among the local basti therapies. The method is the same family; Netra tarpana here focuses on its place among the classical eye procedures, the kriyakalpa.

Classical history

Sushruta Samhita, in its Uttaratantra devoted to eye diseases, and Vagbhata's Ashtanga Hridaya (around 7th century CE) describe the kriyakalpa, the local eye therapies: seka (pouring), aschyotana (eye drops), tarpana (ghee bath), putapaka (herbal juice bath), anjana (collyrium), pindi (poultice) and bidalaka (paste on the lids). For tarpana, they specify how the ring is built, the time of day, the position of the patient, the duration for each eye condition, often measured in matra, a traditional unit of time, and the signs of adequate, excessive and insufficient tarpana. They also list when it should not be done.

Netra tarpana is therefore a classical, documented procedure. Today it is taught and practised in Shalakya Tantra departments across India.

How it is understood to work, and what research shows

Classical reasoning

The eye is described as a seat of alochaka pitta, the fire of vision, and the texts say the eyes are especially threatened by kapha and need protection from heat and dryness. Ghee is described as cooling, nourishing and unctuous, and as able to carry the properties of herbs. Bathing the open eye in warm medicated ghee is believed to nourish the eye and its surrounding tissues, relieve dryness and strain and calm vata and pitta.

Modern research

Netra tarpana has been studied more than many Ayurvedic procedures, though studies remain small. A 2020 open-labelled randomised comparative trial published in Ayu treated 30 patients with dry eye syndrome (shushkakshipaka) with tarpana using either triphala ghrita or goghrita manda, the clear upper part of cow ghee, and reported moderate to marked symptom relief in both groups. A 2017 clinical study in Ayu looked at aschyotana, tarpana and oral medicines in primary open-angle glaucoma, as an adjunct to standard care, and a 2019 case report described Ayurvedic management, including local procedures, in ulcerative keratitis. These studies are small and often lack blinding or placebo comparison. They support tarpana as a reasonable supportive therapy for dry, strained eyes but do not show that it treats glaucoma, cataract or other serious eye disease.

Who may benefit, after an eye examination

  • Dry, gritty, burning eyes, including from long screen use
  • Eye strain and fatigue
  • Some cases of computer vision syndrome, alongside proper glasses and screen habits
  • As supportive care alongside an ophthalmologist's treatment in selected chronic conditions

Who must not have it

  • Acute red eye, eye infection, conjunctivitis or discharge
  • Eye injury, foreign body or chemical splash, which need emergency care
  • Recent eye surgery, including cataract or LASIK, unless your eye surgeon approves
  • Sudden loss or blurring of vision, eye pain, haloes or flashes, which need urgent ophthalmology
  • Acute cold, fever, indigestion or heavy kapha signs, as the texts advise
  • Contact lens wearers must remove lenses and follow advice before and after

Netra tarpana does not replace glasses, eye drops, glaucoma medication or surgery. Never stop prescribed eye drops. Diabetics and people with high blood pressure should have regular eye check-ups with an ophthalmologist regardless of any Ayurvedic treatment.

Netra Tarpana at Sabka Ved Wellness

Consultation

Dr. Aditya Kunal asks about your eye symptoms, screen hours, glasses, eye drops, diabetes and blood pressure, and reviews any recent eye check-up. If you have not had one, or if there are any warning signs, you will be advised to see an ophthalmologist first. He also asks about digestion, sleep and acidity, which Ayurveda links closely with burning and strained eyes.

Preparation

Fresh black gram dough is prepared. The prescribed ghee is gently melted to a lukewarm temperature in a water bath and checked carefully, because the eye is extremely sensitive.

The procedure, for understanding

  1. You lie on your back in a quiet room. The face may be lightly massaged and warmed.
  2. The dough ring is built around one or both eyes and sealed so that it does not leak.
  3. Lukewarm ghee is poured slowly into the ring, over the closed eye, until the lashes are covered.
  4. You are asked to open and close the eyes gently and slowly. A mild blur and warmth are normal.
  5. After the set time, the ghee is drained through an opening near the outer corner, the ring is removed and the eyes are cleaned with warm water or a mild decoction.
  6. You rest with eyes closed before leaving, and may be advised to avoid bright light for a while.

Please do not put ghee or anything else into your eyes at home without a specific prescription.

Aftercare

Vision may be blurred for a short time, so do not drive immediately afterwards. Avoid screens, bright sunlight, dust and wind for the rest of the day. Wear sunglasses outdoors. Sleep on time. Eat light, warm food and include cooling items such as amla and seasonal greens if your digestion allows.

Why it costs what it does

Netra tarpana uses quality medicated ghee, fresh dough for each session, single-patient cotton and linen and up to 45 minutes of close, careful attention, because there is no margin for error with the eye. The doctor's examination and supervision are central. We do not publish fixed prices, but you will know what your course includes before it begins.

Screen habits that help

Any eye therapy works better with simple habits: the 20-20-20 rule (every 20 minutes, look 20 feet away for 20 seconds), blinking consciously, keeping screens at arm's length and slightly below eye level, reducing night-time phone use, and getting your glasses checked. In Patna's dusty summers and smoky winters, splashing the closed eyes with clean, cool water after coming home is a simple classical habit worth keeping, and so is avoiding rubbing the eyes. Children preparing for exams deserve the same care, because many spend more hours on screens than adults do and rarely complain until symptoms are strong.

FAQ

Questions about Netra Tarpana

What is Netra Tarpana?

Netra tarpana is a classical Ayurvedic eye procedure in which lukewarm medicated ghee is held over the open eye inside a ring of black gram dough for a set time.

Does Netra Tarpana help dry eyes?

A small randomised trial published in 2020 reported symptom relief in dry eye syndrome with tarpana. It is used as supportive care, and evidence is still limited.

Can Netra Tarpana remove my glasses or cure cataract?

No. It does not correct refractive errors or treat cataract or glaucoma. Keep using glasses and prescribed eye drops.

Is Netra Tarpana painful?

It should not be painful. You will feel warmth and a mild blur. Any pain or stinging should be reported at once.

Can I drive after Netra Tarpana?

Vision may be blurred for a while, so it is best not to drive immediately afterwards.
