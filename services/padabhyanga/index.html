Padabhyanga in Patna

Padabhyanga is the Ayurvedic oil massage of the feet and lower legs, a classical daily practice described in Charaka Samhita. At a clinic it is done with a chosen medicated oil, sometimes with a bronze bowl (kansa), and is used for relaxation, sleep support, dry and cracked feet, and tired, aching legs.

Think of the people in Patna who stand all day: shopkeepers in busy markets, teachers, nurses on long hospital shifts, traffic police at Gandhi Maidan, mothers who never seem to sit down. For many of them the day ends in the feet, with heaviness, burning soles, cracked heels and calves that ache at night. Ayurveda paid unusual attention to the feet. Padabhyanga, the oil massage of the feet, is one of a handful of practices that the classical texts recommend as part of everyday life, not only as a treatment.

What Padabhyanga is

Pada means foot and abhyanga means oil massage. Padabhyanga is the application and massage of warm oil, or sometimes ghee, over the soles, heels, toes, ankles and lower legs. In many traditions, particularly in north and west India, a small bronze bowl called a kansa vati is rubbed over the soles after oiling. The therapy is short, comfortable and suitable for most adults.

Classical history

Charaka Samhita, in the Sutrasthana chapter on daily regimen, gives a specific passage on oiling the feet. In traditional terms it says that foot massage removes roughness, stiffness, dryness, fatigue and numbness of the feet, gives strength and steadiness to the feet, brightens the eyes, calms vata, and helps prevent cracked feet and constriction of the vessels and ligaments. The same text says it supports sound sleep. Ashtanga Hridaya (around 7th century CE) advises that the head, ears and feet receive oil in particular during daily abhyanga.

The link between the feet and the eyes is a recurring theme in the classics, and Ayurvedic eye care still recommends foot oiling. Kansa (bronze) bowl massage is a widely followed regional practice; it is popular in Ayurvedic clinics today, but we have not found a precise classical text that describes the bowl method itself, so we treat it as tradition rather than classical prescription.

How it is understood to work, and what evidence shows

The Ayurvedic view

Vata is said to be most active in the lower body and in the bones and joints of the feet. Dryness, cracking, cold feet, restless legs and light sleep are classic vata signs. Warm oil and gentle pressure counter dryness and coldness and are believed to steady the mind. For pitta-type burning feet, cooling oils or ghee are used. The feet also carry several marma points that the texts consider important.

What modern research says

Studies of padabhyanga are still mostly small or in the planning stage. A 2026 trial protocol in Frontiers in Medicine is testing cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia with added shiro pichu and padabhyanga in perimenopausal women, which shows that researchers consider it worth testing for sleep, but results are not yet available. A 2022 paper in the Journal of Interprofessional Education and Practice described an Ayurvedic approach to caring for the feet of healthcare workers, who stand for long shifts. Foot massage in general is often used for relaxation, and many people find it calming. Claims that it treats eye disease, diabetes or neuropathy are not established, and we do not make them.

Who may benefit

  • People who stand or walk for long hours and end the day with tired, aching feet
  • Dry, rough or cracked heels, especially in winter
  • Burning feet linked with heat, after medical causes are checked
  • Restless sleep and difficulty winding down at night
  • Elderly people with dry skin and stiff ankles, as gentle care
  • As part of a recovery plan after Panchakarma

Cautions and contraindications

  • Diabetes with neuropathy or poor circulation: feet must be examined first, pressure kept gentle and oil temperature checked carefully, because reduced sensation means burns or injuries can go unnoticed
  • Open cuts, ulcers, fungal infection between the toes, or infected cracks
  • Swelling of one leg, pain in the calf, or suspected deep vein thrombosis: this needs urgent medical evaluation, and massage must be avoided
  • Recent fracture, sprain or foot surgery
  • Fever, acute cold or indigestion, when abhyanga in general is postponed
  • Pregnancy: gentle foot massage is often comfortable, but some points and oils are avoided, so consult first

Burning or numb feet can signal diabetes, vitamin B12 deficiency, thyroid problems or nerve disease. Get them checked. Padabhyanga supports comfort; it does not treat these conditions. Continue your medicines.

Padabhyanga at Sabka Ved Wellness

Consultation

Dr. Aditya Kunal asks about your work, footwear, sleep and any conditions like diabetes or thyroid disease, and examines the feet if there are cracks, swelling or changes in sensation. The oil is chosen accordingly: warming and nourishing for dry, cold feet, cooling for burning feet, or ghee in some cases.

The session, for understanding

  1. You sit or lie comfortably with the legs supported.
  2. The feet and lower legs are cleaned with warm water and dried.
  3. Warm oil is applied from the knees down to the toes.
  4. The therapist massages the calves and ankles, then the soles, heels and each toe, using steady strokes and gentle pressure.
  5. If suitable, a kansa bowl is rubbed in slow circles over the soles.
  6. Excess oil is wiped off, and you rest briefly before walking, to avoid slipping.

Aftercare

Drink a glass of warm water after the session. Wear clean socks or cotton slippers afterwards to keep oil off the floor and protect the feet. Avoid walking barefoot on cold floors right after the session. Light, warm dinner and an early night make the most of the calming effect. If you are on a course, try to keep similar timings each day, because a steady routine is itself part of how Ayurveda calms vata.

Cracked heels: a winter story in Bihar

Every winter, from about November to February, dry air, cold floors, open chappals and too little water turn many heels in Patna rough and split. Ayurveda calls this padadari, cracking of the feet, and names vata dryness and walking barefoot as its main causes. Charaka specifically lists the prevention of cracked feet among the benefits of padabhyanga. In practice, a combination works best: warm water soaks, regular oiling at night, cotton socks, closed footwear outdoors and attention to diet and hydration. Deep, painful or bleeding cracks, or cracks in someone with diabetes, should be examined, because they can become infected and may need medicated applications rather than plain oil.

Padabhyanga is one of our simplest therapies. Its cost covers a chosen classical oil or ghee, therapist time, clean towels and linen for each patient, hygienic cleaning of any bowl used, and the doctor's check that it is suitable for you. Fixed prices are not published because plans vary; you will know what is included in advance.

Taking it home

For most healthy adults, a few minutes of foot oiling at bedtime is a safe daily habit, and Charaka would approve. Use an oil the doctor suggests, warm it slightly, massage the soles and heels, and wear cotton socks to bed. People with diabetes should check their feet daily and follow their doctor's advice about foot care before starting any routine.

FAQ

Questions about Padabhyanga

What is Padabhyanga?

Padabhyanga is the Ayurvedic oil massage of the feet and lower legs, recommended in Charaka Samhita as part of the daily routine.

Is Padabhyanga good for sleep?

Classical texts link foot massage with sound sleep, and many people find it calming. Research is still at an early stage, with trials planned rather than completed.

Can diabetics have Padabhyanga?

Often yes, with care. The feet should be examined first, pressure kept gentle and oil temperature checked, because reduced sensation can hide injuries or burns.

What is the kansa bowl used for?

Many centres rub a small bronze bowl over the oiled soles as part of the massage. It is a popular tradition, though the bowl method itself is not clearly described in the classical texts.

Can I do Padabhyanga at home?

Most healthy adults can oil their feet at bedtime using an oil the doctor suggests. People with diabetes or foot problems should take medical advice first.
