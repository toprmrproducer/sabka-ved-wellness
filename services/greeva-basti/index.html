Greeva / Manya Basti in Patna

Greeva basti, also called manya basti, is an external therapy where warm medicated oil is pooled over the back of the neck inside a black gram dough ring and kept at a steady warmth. It is used for chronic neck stiffness and pain, including age-related changes in the cervical spine, alongside posture correction and exercise, after a doctor has checked for warning signs.

The phone in the hand, the laptop on the bed, the textbook on the lap, the long scooter ride with a helmet in Patna traffic: modern neck pain has many authors. By the time people consult us, many have tried pain balms, pillows and painkillers. Greeva basti is often part of the conversation, and it can be genuinely relieving, as long as it comes with an honest understanding of what it can and cannot do.

What greeva basti means

Greeva (ग्रीवा) means neck, and manya (मन्या) refers to the back and sides of the neck, the region of the nape. Basti here means a reservoir. Greeva or manya basti is the pooling of warm medicated oil over the back of the neck and upper shoulders, inside a ring of black gram dough, for a set period.

History, plainly stated

Like kati and janu basti, greeva basti in its current form is a later clinical adaptation of the classical shiro basti, the retention of oil on the head described in Vagbhata's Ashtanga Hridaya. It is not described as a separate procedure in the Charaka Samhita or Sushruta Samhita. The texts do describe manyastambha, stiffness of the neck attributed to vata and kapha, and treatments involving oleation, sudation and nasya. The dough-ring method on the neck developed in Indian clinical practice and is now included in standard Panchakarma teaching.

How it is understood to work

The Ayurvedic view

Neck stiffness and pain are attributed mainly to vata, especially when the pain is dry, cracking and worse in cold, and to vata with kapha when there is heaviness and morning stiffness. Holding warm oil over the area is believed to lubricate and warm the tissues and calm vata locally. Because the neck is close to the head, the texts' broader treatment of the region often adds nasya, and our plans frequently combine the two. Oils such as mahanarayana taila, ksheerabala taila, karpasasthyadi taila and dhanwantaram taila are commonly chosen.

What evidence exists

Specific research on greeva basti is small-scale, usually single-arm or combined with nasya and internal medicines, so its independent contribution is hard to measure. A randomised trial on the related lower-back therapy, kati basti, found that adding it to yoga did not significantly improve pain beyond yoga alone, which is a useful reminder that exercise and posture are central. Local warmth and gentle massage can ease muscle tension in the short term, and patients often report relief. We present greeva basti as a supportive therapy within a plan, not as a proven treatment for cervical spondylosis.

In the winter months, when cold mornings tighten the neck and shoulders, many patients find warm oil therapies particularly comforting, while in summer the doctor may shorten sessions or pair them with cooler oils.

Who may benefit

  • Chronic neck stiffness and pain related to posture, screen use and muscle tension.
  • Age-related changes in the cervical spine (cervical spondylosis) with stable symptoms, after examination and review of any scans.
  • Shoulder and upper back tension linked to neck strain.
  • Some tension-type headaches arising from the neck, as judged by the doctor.

When greeva basti is not appropriate

  • Neck pain with red flags: weakness, numbness or clumsiness in the arms or hands, difficulty walking, loss of bladder control, severe headache with fever or neck rigidity, pain after an accident or fall, or unexplained weight loss. These need urgent medical evaluation.
  • Dizziness or blackouts on turning the neck, which need assessment before any neck treatment.
  • Acute injury or sudden severe pain.
  • Skin infection, wounds, rashes or burns on the neck.
  • Uncontrolled high blood pressure or significant heart disease, for heat-based sessions.
  • Fever, acute cold with congestion, or the classical ama state.
  • Allergy to black gram or an oil ingredient.

Continue any prescribed medicines unless your prescribing doctor changes them.

What happens at Sabka Ved Wellness

Assessment

Dr. Aditya Kunal examines your neck movement, posture, shoulder muscles and arm reflexes and sensation, and reviews any X-ray or MRI. He asks about your work set-up, screen use, pillow and sleep position, because these often explain why pain keeps coming back.

The session, described for understanding

  1. You lie face down with the forehead supported on a cushion or face rest, so that the neck is straight and relaxed.
  2. A ring of black gram dough is shaped over the back of the neck, from the base of the skull toward the upper back, and sealed.
  3. Warm medicated oil, temperature-checked, is poured into the ring.
  4. As it cools, the oil is gradually replaced with warmer oil, keeping the temperature steady.
  5. After the set time, oil and dough are removed, and the neck and shoulders are gently massaged.
  6. Local nadi sweda or a warm towel may follow.
  7. You sit up slowly, without jerking the neck.

After the session

Keep the neck covered and avoid cold drafts, air-conditioning blowing directly on the neck and head bathing in cold water on treatment days. Avoid long periods of looking down at the phone. Practise the neck movements and posture changes you are shown.

The habits that decide long-term results

For most people with chronic neck pain, the biggest gains come from small, repeated changes: raising the screen to eye level, taking breaks every 30 to 45 minutes, using a pillow that keeps the neck in line with the spine, strengthening the muscles of the upper back and avoiding carrying heavy bags on one shoulder. Students preparing for competitive exams, a large group in Patna, are especially prone to neck strain from long hours of study, and simple study posture changes help a great deal. Greeva basti eases the stiffness; habits keep it from returning.

Patients who travel to Patna from nearby districts for a short stay sometimes ask whether a few days of greeva basti are worthwhile. A short course can ease stiffness, but the exercises and posture changes you take home matter more, so the doctor spends part of the consultation teaching you what to continue after the sessions end.

Greeva basti with nasya

Because Ayurveda treats the neck, head and shoulders as a connected region, the doctor may add nasya, medicated nasal drops given after face and neck massage, to a greeva basti course. This combination is common in classical-style plans for neck stiffness. It is decided individually, since nasya has its own contraindications.

What the cost covers

Each session uses fresh dough and a generous quantity of medicated oil kept warm throughout, a therapist present for the whole session, fresh linen, and doctor review across the course. We do not publish prices online. Your plan and its costs are explained at consultation.

An honest summary

Greeva basti is not a cure for cervical spondylosis or disc disease. It is a soothing, supportive therapy that may help as part of a plan that includes posture, exercise and, where needed, other treatment. FAQ

Questions about Greeva / Manya Basti

Is greeva basti good for cervical spondylosis?

It may ease stiffness and pain as part of a plan that includes posture correction and exercise. It does not reverse age-related changes in the spine.

What if I have tingling in my hands?

Tingling, numbness or weakness in the arms needs proper medical evaluation. Tell the doctor; greeva basti is considered only after warning signs are assessed.

How many sessions are needed?

Commonly 7 to 14, often combined with nasya, exercises and other measures, as decided after assessment.

Will I have to lie face down for long?

Yes, for the duration of oil pooling, with the forehead supported on a cushion or face rest. Tell the staff if you are uncomfortable.

Can students or office workers fit this around work?

Usually yes. Sessions take under an hour. Avoid cold drafts and long screen time immediately afterwards.
