जानु बस्ति knee

Janu Basti in Patna

Janu basti is an external Ayurvedic therapy in which warm medicated oil is pooled over the knee inside a ring of black gram dough and kept warm for a set time. It is used for chronic knee stiffness and pain, commonly in age-related wear of the knee, as one part of a plan that includes exercise and weight management, after a doctor's examination.

'Ghutne ka dard' is something almost every family in Bihar knows through a parent or grandparent: the slow climb up the stairs, the hesitation before sitting on the floor for puja, the creak on getting up. Knee osteoarthritis is extremely common in India, more so with age, weight and years of squatting and cross-legged sitting. Janu basti is one of the Ayurvedic therapies people ask about most for it.

What janu basti is

Janu (जानु) means knee, and basti (बस्ति) here means a reservoir or container. Janu basti creates a small reservoir of warm medicated oil around and over the kneecap, held inside a ring of black gram (urad) dough, and keeps it at a steady warmth for a period of time. It is one of the external basti therapies (bahya basti).

A note on history

As with kati basti and greeva basti, the knee version is not described in this specific form in the major classical texts. The classical model is shiro basti, oil retained on the head in a leather cap, described in Vagbhata's Ashtanga Hridaya and elsewhere. Janu basti is a later clinical adaptation of that principle, refined in Indian and particularly Kerala practice and standardised in modern Panchakarma teaching. The condition it is used for is, however, clearly described: the texts discuss sandhigata vata (vata lodged in the joints), with symptoms such as swelling that feels like an air-filled bag, pain on movement and crepitus, which closely resembles what we now call osteoarthritis.

How it is understood to work

Classical reasoning

In sandhigata vata, the lubricating, nourishing quality of the joint (associated with shleshaka kapha) is thought to be depleted, and vata's dryness takes over, causing pain and crackling. Holding warm, unctuous oil over the joint for a long period is believed to nourish and lubricate, warm the tissues and calm vata. Oils commonly chosen include mahanarayana taila, sahacharadi taila, murivenna, dhanwantaram taila and kottamchukkadi taila, depending on whether there is swelling, heat or dryness.

What research has shown

Studies specifically on janu basti are small, mostly single-centre and often lack control groups, and many combine it with internal medicines or other therapies, so its independent effect is unclear. Local heat and oil massage can ease stiffness and pain in the short term, which is consistent with what patients report. For comparison, a randomised trial of leech therapy for knee osteoarthritis published in 2003 found meaningful short-term pain relief compared with topical diclofenac, showing that local Ayurvedic-derived treatments can be studied rigorously; janu basti has not yet had that level of testing. Mainstream evidence for knee osteoarthritis consistently supports exercise, strengthening of the thigh muscles and weight loss, and we build those into every plan.

This is a simplified guide, not a self-diagnosis tool. A knee that is warm and swollen needs medical evaluation first, because heat-based therapy can worsen some inflammatory conditions.

Season also plays a part. Many patients notice their knees are worse in the cold, damp mornings of a Patna winter and during the monsoon. A course of janu basti is often scheduled in these months, when warmth is most welcome, while in peak summer the doctor may prefer shorter sessions or cooler oils. Older patients travelling from nearby districts sometimes prefer a short, intensive course of daily sessions, which can be planned after the first consultation.

Who may benefit

  • Chronic knee stiffness and pain from age-related wear, after examination and, where useful, an X-ray.
  • Knee pain with crepitus and difficulty climbing stairs or sitting on the floor.
  • Some patients with chronic knee pain after old injuries, once healed.
  • As part of a wider Panchakarma plan for vata conditions affecting the joints.

Who should avoid janu basti

  • A hot, red, acutely swollen knee, which may indicate gout, infection or an inflammatory arthritis flare. This needs prompt medical evaluation.
  • Recent knee injury with fresh swelling, suspected ligament tear or fracture.
  • Recent knee surgery, including joint replacement, until the surgeon permits.
  • Skin infection, open wounds, eczema or burns around the knee.
  • Reduced skin sensation, as in diabetic neuropathy, because of burn risk.
  • Deep vein thrombosis or significant varicose veins around the knee.
  • Fever or acute illness, and allergy to black gram or an oil ingredient.

Continue prescribed medicines for pain, diabetes, blood pressure or other conditions unless your prescribing doctor changes them.

What happens at Sabka Ved Wellness

Assessment

Dr. Aditya Kunal examines both knees, checks alignment, swelling, warmth, range of movement and the muscles around the joint, and reviews any X-ray. He considers weight, footwear, daily activity and other joints. He then decides whether janu basti is suitable and what else your plan should include.

The session, described for understanding

  1. You lie on your back with the knee slightly supported so that it is level.
  2. A ring of black gram dough is built around the kneecap and sealed to the skin.
  3. Warm medicated oil, temperature-checked by the therapist, is poured into the ring.
  4. The oil is replaced gradually with warmer oil as it cools, keeping a steady, comfortable warmth.
  5. After the set time, the oil and dough are removed and the knee is gently massaged.
  6. Local steam or a warm herbal bolus may follow, depending on your plan.
  7. You move the knee gently before standing.

After the session

Keep the knee warm and avoid cold water on it for the day. Avoid long periods of squatting, sitting cross-legged on the floor and climbing many stairs on treatment days. Do the exercises you are taught, typically simple thigh-strengthening and range-of-motion movements, because they matter for the long term.

The rest of a knee plan

For most patients, a sensible plan includes several elements beyond janu basti: strengthening the quadriceps and hip muscles, gradual weight reduction if needed, comfortable footwear, using a chair rather than the floor for long sittings, diet changes that support digestion and weight, and sometimes internal Ayurvedic medicines. In selected cases with a congested, painful knee, the doctor may also discuss leech therapy. Janu basti is the comfortable, warm part of this plan. The exercises are the part that carries most of the long-term weight.

Questions families often ask

Many patients with knee osteoarthritis are older adults, and their children ask whether therapy can avoid a knee replacement. The honest answer is that some people manage well for years with conservative care, while others with advanced joint damage may eventually need surgery. Janu basti cannot rebuild cartilage. What it may offer is comfort and easier movement while the rest of the plan does its work. We will tell you plainly if your knee appears to need an orthopaedic opinion.

What the cost covers

Each session uses fresh black gram dough and a generous quantity of medicated oil kept warm throughout, a therapist present for the entire session to manage temperature and prevent burns, fresh linen, and the doctor's planning and review. Treating both knees takes more time and oil. We do not publish prices online. Your plan and its costs are explained at consultation.

In closing

Janu basti is not a cure for arthritis. It is a soothing therapy that may support comfort and mobility within a well-planned programme. To find out whether it suits you or your parent, request an appointment or book a consultation with Dr. Aditya Kunal online, and bring any X-ray reports.

FAQ

Questions about Janu Basti

Can janu basti avoid knee replacement surgery?

It cannot rebuild cartilage or reverse advanced joint damage. Some people manage well with conservative care, while others may eventually need surgery. The doctor will tell you honestly if an orthopaedic opinion is needed.

How many sessions are usually needed?

Commonly 7 to 14 sessions, along with exercises and other measures. The plan is set after examination.

Can both knees be treated at once?

Yes, both knees can be treated in the same session, which takes more time and oil.

Is janu basti suitable for a swollen, hot knee?

No. A hot, red, acutely swollen knee needs medical evaluation for gout, infection or inflammatory arthritis before any heat therapy.

Do I still need to exercise?

Yes. Strengthening the thigh and hip muscles and managing weight are among the most important parts of knee care.
