Viddhakarma in Patna

Viddhakarma is an Ayurvedic parasurgical procedure in which a doctor briefly punctures the skin at specific points with a sterile, single-use needle. It is used mainly for chronic, localised pain and is based on Sushruta's teaching on vyadhana (puncturing). It is always doctor-performed, after screening, under strict sterile conditions.

Patients who hear the word "needle" in an Ayurvedic clinic usually ask the same question first: "Is this acupuncture?" It is not, though the two share the idea that stimulating specific points can influence pain. Viddhakarma comes from the Indian surgical tradition of Sushruta, uses a different reasoning, and is typically a quick puncture rather than needles left in place. It is a procedure for selected people with stubborn local pain, and it belongs entirely in the hands of a trained doctor.

What Viddhakarma is

Viddha means "pierced" or "punctured", and karma means action or procedure. In Viddhakarma, the doctor identifies specific points, often over or near the painful area and sometimes at classically described sites away from it, and pierces the skin briefly with a sterile, disposable hypodermic needle to a shallow, controlled depth. The needle is removed immediately or after a very short time. A drop of blood may appear, which is wiped away. Several points may be treated in one sitting.

Origins: what is classical and what is modern

Sushruta Samhita, the classical text of Indian surgery, describes vyadhana, puncturing, as one of the eight surgical actions (ashtavidha shastra karma). It discusses puncturing in several contexts: siravyadha (puncturing a vein to let blood, a form of raktamokshana), karnavedhana (ear piercing) and the drainage of collections. Sushruta also describes marma, vital points of the body, which surgeons were taught to protect.

The way Viddhakarma is practised today, as a quick needle puncture at specific points for pain, without the aim of letting blood, has been systematised and taught by Ayurvedic surgeons in India in recent decades, drawing on these classical principles. It is honest to say that modern Viddhakarma is a contemporary clinical development grounded in Sushruta's surgical teaching, rather than a procedure described in exactly this form in the classical texts.

How it is understood to work, and what research shows

The Ayurvedic reasoning

Pain, in Ayurveda, is closely linked with vata, and the texts say that there is no pain without vata. Obstruction in the channels (srotas) is thought to trap vata and cause pain and stiffness. Puncturing at specific points is believed to release this obstruction and restore the free movement of vata, relieving pain. The choice of points is based on the site of pain, the classical anatomy of marma and the practitioner's training.

What research says

The scientific evidence is still early. Published work consists mainly of case reports and small studies. A 2024 case report in the Journal of Pharmacy and Bioallied Sciences described Viddhakarma in a patient with polycystic ovary syndrome, and a 2025 case report in the same journal described its use, with other treatments, in androgenetic alopecia. A 2022 uncontrolled trial in the Journal of Ayurveda and Integrative Medicine reported reduced pain scores after a protocol of parasurgical procedures in chronic musculoskeletal pain. Case reports cannot show that Viddhakarma itself caused improvement, and there are no large controlled trials. We use it carefully, as one option for localised chronic pain, and make no cure claims.

Who may be considered

  • Chronic localised pain in the heel, knee, shoulder, elbow or neck
  • Sciatica-type pain without red flags, as part of a wider plan
  • Muscle stiffness and tender points that have not responded to simpler therapies
  • Some patients who cannot take regular painkillers for long periods, in consultation with their physician

Who should not have Viddhakarma

  • Bleeding disorders, very low platelet counts, or blood thinners, unless reviewed by the treating physician
  • Uncontrolled diabetes, immune suppression, or any condition that raises infection risk
  • Pregnancy
  • Skin infection, eczema, psoriasis or wounds at the puncture sites
  • Severe needle phobia or a history of fainting with needles, unless managed carefully
  • Pain with leg weakness, loss of bladder or bowel control, fever, unexplained weight loss or a history of cancer, which needs urgent investigation
  • Very weak or elderly patients, depending on assessment

Viddhakarma must never be attempted at home or by untrained people. Needle procedures carry real risks of infection, bleeding and injury to nerves and vessels if done incorrectly.

What happens at Sabka Ved Wellness

Consultation and screening

Dr. Aditya Kunal takes a full history, examines the painful area and reviews any imaging and blood reports. He checks your medicines, especially blood thinners, diabetes control and any history of fainting. He explains what the procedure involves, the possible side effects such as brief pain, a drop of blood, small bruises or rarely infection, and the alternatives. Viddhakarma goes ahead only if it is appropriate and you agree after understanding it.

The procedure, for understanding

  1. You sit or lie comfortably, and the points are identified and marked.
  2. The skin is cleaned with antiseptic.
  3. A new, sterile, single-use needle is used for you, and disposed of safely afterwards.
  4. The doctor pierces each point quickly to a shallow depth and withdraws the needle.
  5. Any drop of blood is wiped with sterile cotton, and gentle pressure is applied.
  6. You rest for a few minutes, and the doctor may ask you to move the painful part to check the response.

Most people describe a quick prick, similar to a blood test. Fainting is uncommon but possible, which is why you are kept lying or seated and observed.

Aftercare

Keep the puncture sites clean and dry for the rest of the day. Avoid swimming or soaking until the next day. Mild soreness may last a few hours. Contact the centre if you notice spreading redness, swelling, pus or fever. Light, warm meals and avoiding cold, dry and stale foods are advised, in keeping with vata-calming principles. Gentle movement is usually encouraged, but strenuous exercise may be avoided for a day.

Why we are cautious with needle procedures

In recent years, quick "needle therapy" camps and social media videos have made procedures like Viddhakarma look simple and instant. They are not. Points close to nerves, large vessels and joints need anatomical knowledge, and every puncture carries a small risk of infection if sterility slips. Reusing needles, even once, is dangerous and can spread hepatitis B, hepatitis C and HIV. A responsible clinic uses a new sealed needle for every patient, disposes of it in a sharps container, and keeps the procedure under a qualified doctor.

We also think it is important to be realistic. Some patients feel relief soon after a sitting, others gradually, and some not at all. Pain that keeps returning usually has a reason, such as posture, weight, footwear, weak muscles or the wear of a joint, and those causes need attention alongside any procedure.

Why it costs what it does

Viddhakarma is brief, but safe practice has costs: doctor time for examination, explanation and the procedure itself, sterile single-use needles, antiseptics and sterile cotton, safe biomedical waste disposal, and follow-up to assess the response. It is never delegated to a therapist. We do not publish fixed prices, because plans vary, but you will know what is included in advance.

FAQ

Questions about Viddhakarma

What is Viddhakarma?

Viddhakarma is an Ayurvedic parasurgical procedure in which a doctor briefly punctures the skin at specific points with a sterile, single-use needle to relieve chronic local pain.

Is Viddhakarma the same as acupuncture?

No. Viddhakarma comes from Sushruta's surgical tradition and usually involves a quick puncture with immediate withdrawal, while acupuncture comes from Chinese medicine and leaves fine needles in place.

Is Viddhakarma painful?

Most people feel a quick prick similar to a blood test. Mild soreness may last a few hours.

Is Viddhakarma scientifically proven?

Evidence is early, mostly case reports and small uncontrolled studies. It is used as one option for chronic local pain, not as a guaranteed cure.

Is Viddhakarma safe?

When done by a trained doctor with sterile single-use needles after screening, it is generally well tolerated. It is not suitable for people with bleeding disorders, on blood thinners, in pregnancy or with skin infection at the site.
