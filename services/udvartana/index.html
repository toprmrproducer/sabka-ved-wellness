उद्वर्तन herbal powder massage

Udvartana in Patna

Udvartana is a vigorous Ayurvedic massage in which dry or lightly moistened herbal powder is rubbed over the body in upward strokes, against the direction of hair growth. It is a reducing (langhana) therapy used for heaviness, sluggishness and excess kapha and fat, usually as part of a broader metabolic plan with diet and exercise.

Most Ayurvedic massages are slow, oily and soothing. Udvartana is not. It is brisk, dry and invigorating, and many first-time patients describe it as feeling more like a workout for the skin than a massage. That contrast is deliberate. Udvartana belongs to the reducing side of Ayurvedic therapy, and it is used when the body has too much of what oil massage adds: heaviness, sluggishness, oiliness and excess fat.

At Sabka Ved Wellness, where gut and metabolic health is Dr. Aditya Kunal's focus, Udvartana is often discussed by people struggling with weight, so this page is careful to explain what it can realistically do and what it cannot.

What Udvartana means

Udvartana comes from a Sanskrit root meaning "to rub upward" or "to move upward". The therapist rubs herbal powder over the body in strokes that run from the extremities toward the heart and against the direction of hair growth. When the powder is used dry, the practice is sometimes called ruksha udvartana; when it is mixed with a little oil or warm liquid into a coarse paste, it is called snigdha udvartana and is gentler.

Commonly used powders include triphala, kolakulathadi churna (based on horse gram and related grains) and powders containing herbs such as vacha and haridra (turmeric), selected by the doctor for the person's skin and condition.

Classical foundations

Udvartana is part of the classical daily regimen. Ashtanga Hridaya (around 7th century CE), in its chapter on dinacharya, says that udvartana reduces kapha, liquefies medas (fat), gives firmness to the limbs and improves the skin. Charaka Samhita, in its discussion of the two great therapeutic directions, brimhana (nourishing) and langhana (reducing), places dry, rough treatments among the reducing measures used for excess kapha and medas, and in its chapter on obesity (sthaulya) advises dry, reducing therapies together with diet and exercise. Sushruta also describes udvartana and related techniques, such as utsadana (with unctuous paste) and udgharshana (with dry powder), distinguishing their effects.

So Udvartana is a classical, pan-Indian procedure. Kerala practice, where it is often called udwarthanam, has popularised it in wellness settings, but its roots are in the core texts.

How it works: the classical idea and the evidence

Classical reasoning

Kapha and medas share qualities: heavy, cold, oily, slow and stable. The powders used in Udvartana are described as dry, light, rough and warming. Rubbing them over the skin with friction is believed to counter those heavy qualities, open the channels (srotas), reduce oiliness and help mobilise kapha. Because it reduces rather than nourishes, it is the logical opposite of abhyanga and is often given before swedana (sweating) in people for whom oily massage would be inappropriate.

What research has and has not shown

There is limited modern research. A 2018 case study in Ayu described a 42-year-old woman with dyslipidaemia managed with rukshana upakrama, the classical "drying" approach, which included such reducing measures along with internal medicines and diet; it reported improvement in her lipid profile. A single case report cannot show that Udvartana itself lowers cholesterol or weight, and we are not aware of robust trials of Udvartana alone for obesity. What we can say honestly is that Udvartana is a traditional reducing therapy that many patients find energising, and that sustainable weight change depends far more on diet, activity, sleep and medical care than on any massage.

Who may Udvartana suit?

  • People with excess weight and a heavy, sluggish feeling, as part of a full lifestyle and diet plan
  • Those with oily skin and a tendency to lethargy after meals
  • Some people with heaviness and stiffness in the body linked with kapha, after assessment
  • As preparation before swedana in people for whom oil massage is not suitable
  • Some cases of cellulite-type skin texture, for cosmetic comfort only

Who should avoid Udvartana

  • People who are thin, weak, dehydrated or recovering from illness, since reducing therapy depletes further
  • Very dry, sensitive or thin skin, eczema, psoriasis in flare, open wounds, rashes or skin infection
  • Pregnancy and the post-delivery period, unless specifically advised
  • Fever, acute illness, or immediately after a meal
  • Varicose veins or fragile blood vessels in the treated area
  • Children and the very elderly, in its vigorous form

Udvartana is not a treatment for diabetes, thyroid disease or fatty liver. Keep taking the medicines your doctors have prescribed, and keep up any monitoring they advise.

Udvartana at Sabka Ved Wellness

Assessment first

Weight and metabolic concerns usually have several layers: eating pattern, digestion, sleep, stress, activity, thyroid and blood sugar. Dr. Aditya Kunal reviews reports and history before suggesting Udvartana, and usually combines it with dietary changes and, where needed, internal medicines. Sometimes the first step is correcting digestion with deepana and pachana rather than starting external therapy.

Preparation

The selected powder is warmed slightly, sometimes in a dry pan, so that it feels warm on the skin. For snigdha udvartana it is mixed with a little oil or warm liquid.

The session, for understanding

  1. You lie on the treatment table wearing disposable or minimal clothing.
  2. The therapist, or two therapists working together, apply the powder in firm, upward strokes on the legs, arms, back, abdomen and sides, against the direction of hair growth.
  3. The massage continues in sequence through several positions for around 30 to 45 minutes, with pressure adjusted to your comfort.
  4. Excess powder is brushed off.
  5. Steam (swedana) often follows, then a warm bath after a short rest.

Strong friction on fragile skin can cause abrasions; this is not a home scrub technique.

Aftercare and diet

Take a warm bath without soap if advised, so the skin keeps the effect of the powder. Avoid daytime sleep, heavy meals, sweets, fried food and cold drinks on treatment days. Warm water, light meals such as millet roti, moong dal, green vegetables and a daily walk support the purpose of the therapy.

Our local food has helpful options here. Sattu, roasted chana, barley, seasonal saag and lauki are light and filling, while the classical texts would caution against regular heavy evening meals, sweets after dinner, deep-fried snacks with tea and curd at night. Small, consistent changes like these, kept up after the course ends, are what make the difference.

Why it costs what it does

A single Udvartana uses a substantial quantity of quality herbal powder, often two therapists for synchronised strokes, around an hour of treatment and clean-up time, steam and bathing facilities, and fresh linen that needs thorough washing. The doctor's assessment and follow-up ensure that you are not given a depleting therapy you do not need. Prices are not published because plans vary, but what is included is explained before you start.

Realistic expectations

People sometimes arrive hoping Udvartana will "melt" fat. Classical language about liquefying medas is poetic, not a promise of measurable fat loss from massage. In our experience the real value of a Udvartana course is that it comes with a structured plan: regular meals, earlier dinners, less sugar and refined flour, daily movement and better sleep. The therapy can make those weeks feel lighter and more motivated. The plan does the heavy lifting.

FAQ

Questions about Udvartana

What is Udvartana?

Udvartana is an Ayurvedic massage with dry or coarse herbal powder, rubbed over the body in upward strokes against the direction of hair growth. It is a reducing therapy for excess kapha and fat.

Does Udvartana help weight loss?

Classical texts describe it as reducing kapha and fat, but there is no strong evidence that massage alone reduces weight. It is used as one part of a plan with diet, exercise and medical care.

Is Udvartana painful?

It is brisk and firm but should not be painful. Pressure is adjusted to your comfort, and it is avoided on sensitive or broken skin.

Who should not have Udvartana?

People who are thin or weak, pregnant women, people with skin infections, eczema flares, open wounds, fever or varicose veins should avoid it or consult first.

How many sessions are usually advised?

Commonly 7 to 21 sessions, decided after assessment and alongside diet changes.
