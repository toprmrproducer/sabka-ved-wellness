Uttar Basti in Patna

Uttar basti is the instillation of a small, sterile quantity of medicated oil, ghee or decoction into the bladder through the urethra, or into the uterine cavity through the cervix in women, for selected urological and gynaecological conditions. It is a doctor-performed, sterile procedure that requires investigations, careful selection and informed consent, and it is never offered as a routine or casual therapy.

This page discusses a sensitive procedure. We have written it plainly and clinically, because people considering uttar basti, and the families supporting them, deserve clear information without embarrassment or exaggeration. Uttar basti is performed only by a qualified doctor, in a sterile setting, after investigations and consent. Nothing here is a set of instructions.

What uttar basti is

Uttar (उत्तर) has two meanings that both apply: 'superior' or 'higher', and 'later'. Uttar basti is given through a route higher than, or anatomically above, the rectal route, and it is classically given after the regular basti course. In men, a small quantity of sterile medicated liquid is introduced into the urinary bladder through the urethra. In women, it may be introduced into the bladder through the urethra, or into the uterine cavity through the cervix (intrauterine uttar basti), depending on the indication.

Its place in the classical texts

The Charaka Samhita, in the Siddhi Sthana, where uttar basti is described among the types of basti, and the Sushruta Samhita, which gives a detailed account in the Chikitsa Sthana, describe uttar basti for disorders of the urinary tract and of the female reproductive system. The texts specify the size of the nozzle for men and women, the volume of medicine, the timing in relation to the menstrual cycle, the use of oil or ghee, and the need for prior cleansing. Vagbhata's Ashtanga Hridaya also describes it. These texts recommend giving intrauterine uttar basti in the days after menstruation ends, which is still the practice today.

Modern Prasuti Tantra and Stri Roga (Ayurvedic obstetrics and gynaecology) departments and Shalya Tantra departments have adapted the procedure with sterile disposable catheters, aseptic technique and modern diagnostic support such as ultrasound and hysterosalpingography (HSG).

How it is understood to work

Ayurvedic reasoning

Ayurveda attributes the functions of urination, menstruation, ejaculation and childbirth to apana vata, the downward-moving aspect of vata located in the pelvis. When apana vata is disturbed, the texts describe urinary difficulty, menstrual disorders and difficulties with conception. Uttar basti delivers medicated oils or ghee directly to the affected organ, which is believed to pacify vata locally and nourish the tissues. Commonly mentioned preparations include phala ghrita, kumari taila, kshara taila preparations and specific decoctions, chosen by the doctor.

Research so far

Most published research comes from Ayurvedic teaching hospitals and is small and uncontrolled. A study published in Ayu in 2010 examined intrauterine uttar basti with Kumari Taila in women with fallopian tube blockage confirmed by HSG and reported that blockage was cleared in a large proportion of the small group, with some pregnancies during short follow-up. The authors themselves described it as a preliminary study. Other single-arm studies on kshara-based oils have been published. Without control groups, these results cannot establish effectiveness, and tubal blockage, infertility and urological conditions all have many causes that need proper medical evaluation. We present uttar basti as a classical option under study, not as an alternative to evaluation by a gynaecologist, urologist or fertility specialist.

Working with your other doctors

Couples trying to conceive are often already under the care of a gynaecologist or fertility centre, and men with urinary complaints may be seeing a urologist. We see uttar basti as something that can sit alongside that care when appropriate, not as a competitor to it. It helps if you bring your reports, including semen analysis for the male partner where relevant, since infertility is a shared concern and male factors are common. If an investigation suggests a problem that needs surgery, medication or assisted reproduction, Dr. Aditya Kunal will say so plainly. Delaying necessary specialist treatment in the hope that a classical procedure alone will be enough is not in anyone's interest, especially when age affects fertility.

Who may be considered

After full assessment and investigations, uttar basti may be considered by the doctor in selected cases such as:

  • Some women with infertility in whom tubal factors or other conditions have been evaluated, alongside or after specialist advice.
  • Selected menstrual disorders where the doctor judges a local therapy appropriate.
  • Selected urinary complaints in men and women, such as certain chronic bladder symptoms, after urological evaluation has ruled out conditions that need other treatment.

Who must not have uttar basti

  • Any possibility of pregnancy; a pregnancy test is done before intrauterine uttar basti.
  • Active urinary tract infection, vaginal or cervical infection, pelvic inflammatory disease or sexually transmitted infection.
  • During menstruation or with abnormal vaginal bleeding that has not been investigated.
  • Suspected or known cancer of the cervix, uterus, bladder or prostate.
  • Urethral stricture, bladder stones or prostate enlargement causing obstruction, unless evaluated and specifically planned.
  • Recent surgery or instrumentation of the urinary or reproductive tract.
  • Uncontrolled diabetes, immune suppression or fever.
  • Intrauterine uttar basti is generally not given to young girls or to women who have not been sexually active, in line with usual clinical practice.
  • Anyone who has not given fully informed consent.

Never stop hormonal treatment, fertility medication or any prescribed medicine without your specialist's advice. We are happy to coordinate with your gynaecologist or urologist.

What happens at Sabka Ved Wellness

Consultation and investigations

Dr. Aditya Kunal takes a detailed, private history and reviews investigations, which may include ultrasound, HSG report, urine tests, blood sugar, and pregnancy test where relevant. He explains the procedure, its alternatives, the possible discomfort and risks, and answers questions from you and your spouse if you wish. Written consent is taken. Patients for whom uttar basti is not appropriate are told so clearly.

Preparation

The classical texts advise some preparatory cleansing beforehand, usually one or two rectal bastis. For intrauterine uttar basti, the procedure is timed in the days after menstruation has stopped and before ovulation. You are asked to empty your bladder before the procedure.

The procedure, described for understanding

  1. The procedure is done in a private room with a female attendant present for female patients and a male attendant for male patients.
  2. Mild abhyanga and warmth to the lower abdomen and back may be given first.
  3. The area is cleaned with antiseptic, and the doctor uses sterile gloves, sterile drapes and a sterile single-use catheter.
  4. A small, measured amount of warm, sterile medicated oil or ghee is gently introduced into the bladder or uterine cavity, depending on the indication.
  5. The catheter is removed, and you lie with the hips slightly raised for some time.
  6. You rest and are observed before leaving.

Aftercare

Mild discomfort, a feeling of fullness or slight spotting may occur. Abstinence from intercourse and avoidance of heavy exertion are advised for the period the doctor specifies. Fever, significant pain, burning on urination, heavy bleeding or foul discharge must be reported immediately.

Why we treat uttar basti with extra caution

Any procedure that enters the bladder or uterus carries a risk of infection and injury if done carelessly. That is why it is never delegated, never done without investigations and sterile materials, and never offered as part of a package to people who have not been individually assessed.

What the cost covers

Sterile single-use catheters and drapes, sterile medicated preparations, investigations, the doctor's direct time, a private procedure room, and preparatory and follow-up care make up the cost of uttar basti. It is usually given over a few days in one or more cycles. We do not publish prices online; the plan and its costs are explained at consultation.

An honest closing

Uttar basti is not a cure for infertility, tubal blockage or any urological disease, and no one can promise conception. It is a classical procedure that may have a place for some patients after proper evaluation. If you would like to discuss it privately, request an appointment or book a consultation with Dr. Aditya Kunal online, and bring all relevant reports.

FAQ

Questions about Uttar Basti

Is uttar basti painful?

Most patients describe mild discomfort or pressure rather than pain. The doctor uses a fine sterile catheter and works gently. Tell the doctor if you feel significant pain at any point.

When in the cycle is uterine uttar basti given?

Classically and in current practice, in the days after menstruation has stopped and before ovulation, after a pregnancy test where relevant.

Can uttar basti guarantee pregnancy?

No. No one can guarantee conception. Uttar basti is a classical procedure under study, and infertility needs proper evaluation of both partners.

Is uttar basti done for men?

Yes. In men it is given into the bladder through the urethra for selected urinary conditions, after urological evaluation.

Will a female attendant be present?

Yes. For female patients, a female attendant is present throughout, and privacy and dignity are maintained.
