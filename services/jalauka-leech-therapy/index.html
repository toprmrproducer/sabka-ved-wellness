जलौकावचारण leech therapy

Jalaukavacharana in Patna

Jalaukavacharana is the application of medical leeches to a specific site so that they draw a small amount of blood and release saliva containing natural anticoagulant and anti-inflammatory substances. It is the gentlest form of classical bloodletting and is used for selected local conditions, only after a doctor's assessment and blood tests, with single-use leeches.

Few treatments make people flinch at first mention as much as leeches do. Then they learn two things. The first is that the bite is often less painful than an injection. The second is that modern plastic and reconstructive surgeons in many countries use medical leeches to save surgically reattached fingers and skin flaps from congestion. The creature is the same one Sushruta described more than two thousand years ago, and the logic, removing stagnant blood from a congested area, is not as far apart as it might seem.

What the name means

Jalauka (जलौका) means 'one whose home is water', the leech. Avacharana (अवचारण) means application. Jalaukavacharana is therapy by leech application, one of the methods of raktamokshana (therapeutic bloodletting) classed as anushastra, a para-surgical method that does not need a blade.

History: an unusually well-recorded tradition

The Sushruta Samhita contains a full chapter on leeches (Sutra Sthana, chapter 13). It describes twelve types, six venomous and six non-venomous, explains how to recognise them, where to collect them, how to keep them in clean water with suitable food, how to prepare them before use, how to apply them, how to make them detach and how to make them vomit the ingested blood so that they are not reused while full. It recommends leeches especially for pitta-type blood disorders and for patients who are delicate: children, the elderly, women and the timid, for whom other methods would be too harsh.

Leech therapy was also used in Greek, Arabic and European medicine. In Europe it was overused in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries and fell out of favour. In the twentieth century, research on leech saliva led to the isolation of hirudin, a powerful anticoagulant, and medicinal leeches returned to some hospital surgical units for venous congestion. In India, Ayurvedic hospitals have kept jalaukavacharana in continuous use.

How it is understood to work

The Ayurvedic view

Leeches are described as cold in nature and therefore especially suited to pitta and rakta disorders, where heat, redness and inflammation dominate. They are believed to draw out the vitiated blood selectively from the area while sparing healthy blood. This is why they are applied directly to the affected site.

The scientific picture

When a medicinal leech bites, it releases saliva containing more than a hundred bioactive substances, including hirudin (anticoagulant), as well as compounds with anti-inflammatory, vasodilating and local anaesthetic effects. The bite continues to ooze for hours after the leech detaches because of this anticoagulant effect, which extends the local decongestion.

The best-known clinical study is a randomised controlled trial by Michalsen and colleagues published in the Annals of Internal Medicine in 2003. In 51 patients with knee osteoarthritis, a single application of four to six leeches reduced pain more than a 28-day course of topical diclofenac at seven days, with improvements in function and stiffness persisting for weeks. The trial was not blinded against a sham procedure. A 2022 open-label study from India used leeches within a protocol for chronic musculoskeletal pain and reported improvement without adverse events, but had no control group. The evidence is promising for some painful conditions and incomplete overall.

Myths we hear about leeches

'Leeches suck out all the bad blood in the body.' No. A leech draws a small amount of blood from a local area. Its effect is local decongestion plus the action of its saliva, not a whole-body cleanse.

'Any leech from a pond will do.' No. Sushruta himself warned against venomous and unsuitable leeches. Wild leeches can carry infection. Only clean, medically suitable leeches from reliable sources should be used, and only once per patient.

'Leech therapy is painful and dangerous.' For most people, the bite is a brief sting. The real risks are prolonged bleeding, infection and allergic reactions, which is why selection, blood tests, sterile dressings and observation matter.

'More leeches mean better results.' No. The number is chosen for the site and your strength. More is not better, and excessive blood loss is a recognised complication.

Leeches and modern medicine

It surprises many patients that medicinal leeches are approved for use in some countries' hospitals to relieve venous congestion after reconstructive surgery, and that hirudin-derived anticoagulants are part of mainstream pharmacology. That history does not prove every traditional use, but it shows the leech is a biologically active treatment, which is precisely why it must be used with care.

Who may be considered

  • Some painful joint conditions such as knee osteoarthritis, after examination.
  • Selected chronic skin lesions, local inflammatory swellings and abscess-prone areas, where appropriate.
  • Some varicose vein related symptoms and venous congestion, with careful assessment of the veins.
  • Selected non-healing wounds, as an adjunct to proper wound care.

Who must not receive leech therapy

  • Anaemia, bleeding disorders, low platelets, haemophilia.
  • People on anticoagulants or antiplatelet drugs, unless their prescribing doctor and ours agree it is safe.
  • Pregnancy, breastfeeding and during menstruation.
  • Immune suppression, including chemotherapy, uncontrolled diabetes or HIV with low counts, because of infection risk.
  • Arterial disease or poor circulation in the limb.
  • Known allergy to leech saliva, or a previous severe reaction.
  • Very weak or emaciated patients and children, unless specifically decided.
  • Strong fear or aversion to leeches.

Medicines are never stopped on your own. If you take blood thinners, your prescribing doctor must be involved.

What happens at Sabka Ved Wellness

Before the sitting

Dr. Aditya Kunal examines the area, reviews your history and asks for a recent complete blood count, and sometimes clotting tests and blood sugar. He decides how many leeches, which site and how many sittings.

The sitting, described for understanding

  1. The skin is washed and cleaned; perfumed soaps or creams are avoided beforehand because leeches may not attach to them.
  2. Medical-grade leeches are prepared in fresh water with a little turmeric, as the texts describe.
  3. The doctor places each leech on the site. If it does not attach, a tiny prick may be used to draw a drop of blood.
  4. Once attached, the leech is covered with wet gauze to keep it moist. You may feel a small sting at first, then little or nothing.
  5. The leech feeds for roughly 20 to 60 minutes and detaches on its own. If needed, a pinch of turmeric is sprinkled on its mouth to release it; leeches are never pulled off.
  6. The bite is allowed to ooze briefly, then cleaned, turmeric powder applied, and a sterile dressing placed.
  7. You rest and are observed before leaving.

Aftercare

Some oozing from the bite for several hours is expected. Keep the dressing on, change it as advised, and avoid scratching. Mild itching around the bite in the following days is common. Report persistent heavy bleeding, spreading redness, pus, fever or dizziness immediately.

Single use, always

Leeches used for one patient are never applied to another person. This is a non-negotiable safety rule, because of the risk of transmitting infection through blood. Clinics that reuse leeches between patients are taking a risk we will not take.

Why leech therapy costs what it does

Medical-grade leeches have to be sourced from clean suppliers, kept in proper conditions and used for one patient only. Each sitting needs a doctor's time for the entire procedure, sterile dressings and observation afterwards, and blood tests come first. We do not publish prices online; the plan and costs are explained at consultation.

Final word

Leech therapy is not a cure for arthritis, varicose veins, psoriasis or any disease. For carefully selected patients, it is a classical treatment with some encouraging modern evidence. FAQ

Questions about Jalaukavacharana

Does a leech bite hurt?

Most people feel a small sting when the leech attaches, then very little. Some itching around the bite in the following days is common.

Are the leeches reused?

Never for another patient. Leeches are used for one person only, which is an essential infection-control rule.

Why does the bite keep bleeding after the leech falls off?

Leech saliva contains anticoagulants such as hirudin, so mild oozing for several hours is expected. Heavy, persistent bleeding should be reported immediately.

Is leech therapy safe with blood thinners?

Usually not. Blood thinners increase bleeding risk. Do not stop them yourself; your prescribing doctor must be involved in any decision.

How many sittings are usually needed?

Often between one and six sittings, about a week apart, depending on the condition and response.
