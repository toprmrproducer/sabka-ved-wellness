नस्य / शिरोविरेचन nasal therapy

Nasya / Shirovirechana in Patna

Nasya is the administration of medicated oils, ghee, herbal juices or powders through the nostrils, after facial massage and steam, to act on the head, sinuses and neck region. It ranges from gentle nourishing drops to stronger cleansing forms (shirovirechana), and the type and dose are decided by the doctor after assessment.

'The nose is the doorway to the head.' This line, repeated in nearly every classical text on nasya, explains why Ayurveda treats the nostrils as a route for medicine rather than simply a passage for air. For conditions of the head, sinuses, eyes, ears, neck and shoulders, the texts favour nasya as the principal cleansing procedure, in the same way vamana is for the chest and virechana for the abdomen.

What nasya means

Nasya (नस्य) means relating to the nose, from nasa, nose. It is any medicine given through the nostrils. Shirovirechana (शिरोविरेचन), meaning 'purgation of the head', is the stronger, cleansing form of nasya and is counted among the five Panchakarma procedures in the Charaka tradition.

Classical description and types

The Charaka Samhita discusses nasya in the Sutra Sthana and in the Siddhi Sthana, and Vagbhata's Ashtanga Hridaya gives it a full chapter. The texts classify nasya in several overlapping ways:

ClassificationTypesIn simple terms
By actionVirechana (cleansing), brimhana (nourishing), shamana (pacifying)Removing kapha, nourishing dry tissues, or calming a dosha
By formNavana (oil drops), avapeeda (fresh herb juice), dhmapana or pradhamana (powder blown into the nose), dhuma (smoke), pratimarsha and marsha (low and high dose oil)How the medicine is delivered

The best-known nasya oil today, anu taila, is a classical formulation described in Vagbhata's work. Regional variations of technique developed in Kerala and northern India, and standard operating procedures are now taught in Panchakarma departments across the country.

How it is understood to work

The Ayurvedic view

The head (shiras) is considered the seat of the senses and of prana. Kapha tends to accumulate in the head and sinuses, causing heaviness, congestion and dullness, while vata in the head causes dryness, pain and disturbed sleep. Nasya is believed to reach the region through the nasal passages, loosen and drain kapha in cleansing forms, or nourish dry tissues in nourishing forms. Massage and steam on the face before instillation are thought to soften and mobilise the doshas.

What research shows

Studies of nasya are small. A 2020 pilot study in the Journal of Ayurveda and Integrative Medicine compared nasya with Triphaladi oil and a medicated enema in obesity, and the authors suggested nasya might be a practical option worth further study. Small trials in cervical spondylosis, migraine and chronic sinusitis have been published in Ayurvedic journals with positive results, but they are mostly unblinded and short. Modern science recognises the nasal route for some drugs, but there is no strong evidence that classical nasya reaches the brain in a meaningful way, and we make no such claim. A case report in the literature has also described fever and body ache after nasya, reminding us that it is a procedure with real effects and potential reactions.

Timing, season and dose

The classical texts are precise about when nasya should be given. Cleansing nasya for kapha is favoured in the morning, when kapha is said to predominate, and on clear days rather than cloudy, rainy ones. Nourishing nasya for vata may be given at other times. Rainy, overcast and very cold days are traditionally avoided for strong forms. In Bihar, that means the doctor may adjust the schedule during the monsoon or on days of dense winter fog.

Dose is measured in drops (bindu) and varies widely: a few drops in each nostril for gentle forms, more for marsha nasya, and very small quantities of powder for pradhamana. Stronger forms are never self-administered. The signs of proper nasya listed in the texts include lightness of the head, clear breathing through the nose, clearer senses and sound sleep. Signs of excess include headache, heaviness, a feeling of the head being emptied and dizziness, and signs of insufficient nasya include persistent heaviness and congestion. The staff look for these signs and report them to the doctor, who adjusts the next day's plan.

Who may be considered

  • Head and neck conditions with kapha or vata involvement, as judged by the doctor, such as some chronic sinus congestion, headaches, neck stiffness and certain sleep complaints.
  • Patients in a Panchakarma programme where the head region needs attention.
  • People who may benefit from gentle nourishing nasya, such as pratimarsha, as part of daily care.

When nasya should not be given

The classical contraindications remain relevant:

  • Immediately after a meal, drinking water or alcohol, or right before or after a head bath.
  • Acute cold with fever, acute sinus infection with fever, acute cough or breathing difficulty.
  • Immediately after vamana, virechana, basti or bloodletting.
  • Pregnancy and the period soon after childbirth.
  • Very young children and very elderly people, for stronger forms.
  • Fatigue, hunger, thirst, grief or emotional distress at the time.
  • Nasal polyps with bleeding, recent nasal or sinus surgery, frequent nosebleeds, uncontrolled high blood pressure (for strong cleansing forms).

Never stop nasal sprays, inhalers or other prescribed treatment for asthma, allergy or sinus disease without your prescribing doctor's advice. Tell us everything you use.

What happens at Sabka Ved Wellness

Assessment

Dr. Aditya Kunal examines the nose, sinuses and general health, reviews your history and decides which type of nasya, which medicine and how many drops or what dose, as well as the number of days. Cleansing nasya is not used during acute infection.

The procedure, for understanding

  1. You are asked to come with an empty stomach or a light early breakfast taken well before, and after passing urine and stool.
  2. Oil is massaged over the face, forehead, neck and shoulders.
  3. Gentle steam or warm towels are applied to the face to induce mild sweating.
  4. You lie on your back with the head slightly tilted back and the feet slightly raised.
  5. The measured, warm medicine is instilled into each nostril in turn while the other is closed, and you are asked to inhale gently.
  6. The soles, palms, shoulders and neck may be massaged. Any medicine reaching the throat is spat out, not swallowed.
  7. You rest for a few minutes. Warm water gargles and, in some protocols, herbal smoke inhalation follow to clear residual kapha.

Aftercare

For the rest of the day, avoid cold water, cold drinks, cold wind, dust, smoke, head baths, sleeping in the day and shouting or prolonged talking. Eat light, warm food. Some sneezing, watering of the eyes and mild nasal discharge are common after cleansing nasya. Severe headache, nosebleed or fever should be reported.

Nasya and life in a city like Patna

Winter smog, construction dust, crop-burning haze and seasonal pollen are part of life across the Indo-Gangetic plain, and many patients come to us with congestion and headaches that worsen in these months. Nasya may be part of their plan. It is not a substitute for medical treatment of asthma or infection, and we work alongside your other doctors, not against them.

What goes into the cost

Classical nasya oils such as anu taila are prepared through lengthy repeated processing, and fresh herb juices for avapeeda nasya are made on the day. Each session includes face massage, steam, careful instillation by trained staff, single-use droppers, fresh towels and doctor monitoring. We do not publish prices online. Your plan and its cost are explained at consultation.

In closing

Nasya is not a cure for sinusitis, migraine, spondylosis or any other disease. It is a classical procedure that can be valuable when chosen correctly. If you would like to know whether nasya fits your situation, request an appointment or book a consultation with Dr. Aditya Kunal online.

FAQ

Questions about Nasya / Shirovirechana

Is nasya the same as using nasal drops at home?

No. Clinical nasya follows face massage and steam, uses a medicine and dose chosen for you, and includes specific positioning and aftercare. Gentle daily drops (pratimarsha) are a separate, milder practice that should still be advised by a doctor.

Does nasya burn or hurt?

Cleansing nasya can cause a brief stinging, sneezing and watery eyes. Nourishing nasya is usually mild. Tell the staff if discomfort is strong.

Can I have nasya during a cold?

Strong cleansing nasya is usually avoided during an acute cold with fever. The doctor decides based on your condition on the day.

How many days of nasya are needed?

Commonly 7 days, sometimes 14 or 21, depending on the condition and response.

Should I swallow the oil that reaches my throat?

No. It is spat out, followed by warm water gargles.
